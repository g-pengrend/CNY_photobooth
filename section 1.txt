. Background (Domain: Scalable AI for Retail & Fashion SMEs)
The retail industry is undergoing a shift toward Hyper-Personalisation. The global retail and fashion landscape is currently witnessing this "Phygital" revolution where physical storefronts are merged with digital interactivity. As e-commerce continues to dominate, brick-and-mortar retailers are seeking ways to provide "Experiential Retail" that cannot be replicated online.
The Shift to Personalization: Consumers increasingly expect hyper-personalized experiences. AI-driven "Virtual Try-Ons" (VTON) and digital styling assistants have emerged as the gold standard for engagement. However, high-fidelity AI tools remain restricted to high-budget flagship stores or cloud-based platforms due to the immense computational power required.
The SME Barrier: For Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises (SMEs), these technologies remain largely inaccessible. The "Cloud-Only" model presents a double-edged sword: high recurring subscription costs and significant concerns regarding the privacy of customer facial data. There is a clear market gap for an On-Premise Edge AI solution that is:
1.	Cost-Effective: Runs on a single, one-time investment in consumer-grade hardware.
2.	Privacy-First: Processes all biometric data locally, never sending sensitive images to the cloud.
3.	High-Fidelity: Leverages the latest in Diffusion models to provide a "cinematic" output that competes with enterprise-level solutions.

2. Problem Statement
2.1. Problem Description
The Current State: SMEs in the retail and fashion sectors are currently priced out of high-fidelity AI experiences. Solutions that offer realistic "Virtual Try-Ons" or "Cinematic Transformations" are predominantly cloud-dependent, requiring high subscription costs and posing data privacy risks for local businesses. Furthermore, generating high-fidelity video on affordable, consumer-grade hardware is computationally heavy, often leading to system crashes or long, unmanaged wait times that cause customer drop-off in a live retail environment.
The Desired State: An on-premise, "Edge AI" installation that operates seamlessly on a single consumer-grade workstation. It should bridge the gap between high-fidelity output and limited hardware by utilizing a user journey that intelligently masks processing latency, transforming a technical limitation into an engaging brand experience.
2.1.2. Purpose Statement
The objective of this project is to develop a robust, local AI-driven kiosk system that utilises deliberate hardware self-constraints to deliver high-fidelity image and video assets. The project seeks to prove that with optimised memory management and strategic UX design (Design Thinking), a single consumer-class GPU can provide a premium, private, and interactive "Virtual Try-On" experience that drives customer engagement for SMEs.
2.1.3. Scope
Included: Development of a "Thin Client" UI (Touchscreen-optimized) and a "Heavy Worker" backend.
•	Implementation of an asynchronous multi-stage workflow (Face-Swap  High-Res Still  Video Animation).
•	Performance profiling, pipeline refactoring, and model-level optimisation to minimise diffusion model generation time. (Target to hit within 120 – 180 seconds)
•	VRAM management techniques to handle sequential model loading on a single 24GB GPU.
•	A "User Journey" design that incorporates data capture and interactive elements to occupy the 120-second (approximated) render window.
Excluded: Cloud-based processing or SaaS integration (strictly on-premise).
•	Real-time live-streamed AR (focus is on high-fidelity generated assets).
•	Custom model training (focus is on utilising and optimising existing open-source models).
2.1.4. Methodology
The project will follow a Modular-Asynchronous Approach (Planned):
•	Backend Architecture: Utilising Flask and Python to act as a state-machine that manages the handshake between the UI and the ComfyUI API.
•	Memory Management: Implementing a "Load-Execute-Unload" sequence for heavy models to stay within the VRAM ceiling of consumer hardware.
•	UX Design: Utilising Design Thinking to map the "Dopamine Bridge", delivering a high-quality static image within 15 seconds to earn "user patience" for the subsequent video generation.
•	Hardware Decoupling: Connecting the frontend (Touchscreen TV) to the backend (GPU Workstation) via a local network (LAN) using WebSockets for real-time progress updates.
2.1.5. Significance of the experiment
This project is significant because it democratises high-end Generative AI for the SME market. By proving that "Edge AI" is viable on consumer hardware, it removes the financial and privacy-related barriers to entry.
•	For the SME: It provides a one-time investment solution that increases foot traffic and brand social-proof without recurring costs.
•	For the Customer: It offers a high-fidelity, personalised digital souvenir while ensuring their biometric data never leaves the local premises.
•	Technical Impact: It serves as a "Buy-In" prototype that demonstrates how software optimisation and clever UX can overcome physical hardware limitations, potentially serving as a blueprint for other local AI applications.
3. Initial Assumptions
•	Consumer-Grade Viability: I assume that prevailing open-source models and optimisation techniques (such as model quantisation) have matured enough to produce commercial-grade results on a single 3090/4090-class GPU.
•	Sequential Workflow & VRAM Bottlenecks: Chaining high-fidelity models (Face-Swap, Video-Animations, etc) often exceeds the 24GB VRAM limit of consumer GPUs. I assume that the memory management will work.
•	Asynchronous UX: It is assumed that the user experience can be designed to handle the processing wait time through a multi-step interaction (e.g., style selection followed by contact info input) via a WebSocket-driven interface.
•	Latency Management: High-fidelity transformations on local hardware involve significant processing time. Retail environment tends to be fast paced, this "dead time" creates a user experience gap that must be bridged with interactive UI elements to prevent customer drop-off. The assumption is that keeping users on this UX journey will be engaging enough for the consumer.
•	The "Buy-In" Strategy: This local deployment serves as a low-friction "Proof of Concept." I assume SMEs prefer a one-time hardware investment over cloud-based SaaS at the start of their AI journey. (Rightfully, should do a market survey)
•	Edge Privacy: By processing all data locally, I assume the solution meets the high privacy standards required by retail clients who are adverse to uploading customer facial data to the cloud.
